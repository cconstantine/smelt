# Which model a turn runs on

Part of [api.md](../api.md). Which provider and model each conversation runs on, its reply budget, cut-off replies and how a turn fails.

Each conversation has its own provider and model (`conversations.provider_id`/`model`, SME-72). `run_turn_body` resolves them into a `providers::TurnModel` (endpoint, model id, thinking, context window) the first time it needs to call the model: after the new message and any pending notices are saved, so both survive a turn that can't run, and not at all when a wake-up finds nothing to do. The whole turn, its tool loop and any compaction use that one snapshot; a change made meanwhile applies from the next turn. That includes the provider's request slots (SME-162, SME-343): once the model is resolved the turn takes one of the endpoint's slots, when the provider has a max concurrent requests, and holds it until the turn ends — every model call it makes, its retries, its tool passes and compaction's own summarization send through that one slot (`turn::call::TurnSlot`, `anthropic::stream::SlotPolicy::Held`) — so a one-slot server is never switched to another conversation between one turn's calls (a switch re-prefills the prompt) and two conversations on it take whole turns. The exception is the turn's own agents (SME-354 B6): while one under the conversation, at any depth, is `starting` or `active` on the same provider, the turn gives its slot up across each tool pass (`TurnSlot::yield_to_own_agents`) and takes one again before its next call, so a child queued on the slot runs between its parent's calls instead of waiting for the parent's whole turn. The turn's wait for its slot is recorded on the turn's span as `smelt.slot_wait_ms`, never on the calls'. Unlike the rest of the snapshot, a change of the max applies at the running turn's next call (review 1): the endpoint names its provider and the turn re-checks its pool at each loop iteration (`providers::limiter`), a new count resizes the provider's one pool in place (review 2), counting the slots already handed out: lowered, it takes back free slots and then the next ones given back, so no new call starts until the running ones are under the new limit, and the turn keeps its slot through a resize; clearing it lets the waiting calls go, and a cleared or replaced pool is one the turn gives its held slot up for at its next refresh — taking the current pool's slot if a limit appeared there in the meantime.

**What the summariser reads** (`turn::compaction_transcript`, SME-386 F11): the latest summary and everything since, cut oldest first to `compaction_transcript_budget`. The user's and the parent's own words (what `typed_user_text` quotes, plus what hasn't been answered yet) and the latest summary are kept ahead of that cut, at most a quarter of the budget each and inside it, so the tail gets at least half. A first compaction that fits whole is sent as it is.

**A compaction has to leave room** (SME-386 F10, `turn::CompactionProgress`, kept in the conversation's `ConversationRuntime`, so it outlasts a turn and holds for the wakes after it): compaction can't shrink the system prompt and tools, so a window near what they take plus `MIN_REPLY_TOKENS` would compact after every step. Each compaction is judged by the input the next reply measures, against the trigger, not by what it freed. Two results in a row leaving under 4,096 tokens of room mean compacting can't help: it's skipped from then on, and the turn carries on, until the request nears the window itself (the window less `COMPACTION_SAFETY_BUFFER` and 2,048 for a short reply), where it has real history to free. Requests past the usual trigger ask for only the reply that fits (`budget_within_room`). A reply one of those requests cut off at the room left, with more than 2,048 tokens a compaction would free, doesn't end the turn: the model is told it was cut, the conversation compacts, and it's called again, once, until a reply finishes or the user writes (SME-386 round 2). A compaction leaving room to work in goes back to the usual trigger. Three compactions in a row leaving under 2,048 tokens below that limit mean the conversation doesn't fit: the turn fails, with a reason naming the window and what a compacted request takes, said at once if a reply was cut off on that result, saved as a failure notice even on a root's wake, and each wake after it fails the same way without calling the model or saving the notice again, until the window changes or the user writes. **The floor** is separate from that count, and can fire on the first result: a request past the usual trigger that the estimate leaves under 1,024 tokens for a reply compacts if it hasn't just (the usage row a compaction clears can leave a big paste looking this way), and is built again. If it has just compacted, the size a reply last measured after a compaction is believed over the estimate when that shows room, until the server refuses a request whose budget it gave: from then the larger of it and the refusal's count is believed (SME-386 final review M-1). Otherwise the turn fails the same way, without a model call. The user's next send lets one request through at 1,024 tokens whatever the floor says, so the server's answer can move things: they always have a way out. While the conversation stays that tight, that costs a summary on every send and a call on every other one, and one whose measured room is between 1,024 and 5,119 tokens (a short reply fits, but not with the estimate's buffer) alternates between a reply and "too small". **The retry after a refusal naming a window** keeps to the room that window leaves, through the floor, whatever's been learned. Without a compaction its budget comes from the larger of the last usage's projection and the server's count; after one, its estimate gains however far the refused request's fell short of that count. "Too small for smelt's system prompt and tools" is said only when they leave no room for a 1,024-token reply by the estimate, without its buffer, and the wakes after it say so without calling, until the window changes or the user writes. `turn/tests/escape.rs` is a property test of all this: random sequences of sends, wakes, refusals, compactions and changes to the window and the tools, after which a send that fits a short reply reaches the server within three sends, and wakes alone stop spending on a conversation that doesn't fit. A turn may compact as often as it needs when each compaction leaves real room, such as one reading big outputs step after step. A compaction answering a refusal counts too, and three of those in a row, with no step the server took between, fail the turn the same way, whatever usage the server reports. A refusal naming no window has the turn guess one, for that turn alone: nothing is learned or failed against the guess, and it's never named to the user. A restart loses the record, which costs a summary or two.

**The reply budget** (SME-111): each request's `max_tokens` is `turn::reply_budget`, computed per request and never stored: the smallest of the model's output cap (where known), the room the request leaves in the context window less `COMPACTION_SAFETY_BUFFER`, and half the window, so a single reply can't crowd the rest of the history out of the next compaction. It's never below `MIN_REPLY_TOKENS` (16,384, the old fixed value) or half a smaller window, except for a request past the trigger because compacting can't help, which gets only the room left (see above); the compaction trigger keeps that much room, so a request that would leave less compacts first, and the budget is then computed on the smaller history. The output cap is the model's "Max reply tokens", else what its provider reported (`provider_models.reported_max_output`). The request's input is the last real usage plus an estimate of what's new (`projected_input`), or, with nothing measured since the history changed shape (a first request, or one just after a compaction), an estimate of the whole request (`estimate_request_tokens`). A model on an Anthropic or Other provider that nothing sized is capped at 16,384 (`providers::UNKNOWN_OUTPUT_CAP`): a Claude model refuses a `max_tokens` above its own cap. A reply that stops with `stop_reason: "max_tokens"`, or `"model_context_window_exceeded"` (Claude 4.5+, the window reached first; the notice then gives the tokens it wrote), is kept, and the turn saves a notice after it (`api::chat::cut_off_notice`, a user-role message like the stop notice, so the model knows its reply is incomplete): every tab shows it live as the transcript's notice line, naming the limit, what bound it (`ReplyLimit`: the model's cap, which "Max reply tokens" can raise; an Anthropic model's own reported cap, which it can't; half the window; or the room left) and what to change. It isn't continued: Claude refuses a prefilled partial reply, and a local model would start its reasoning over. The one time the model is called again is when the room cut it off past the trigger (see above), and that's a new reply, told the last was cut. A reply that stops with `stop_reason: "refusal"` is kept the same way, with a call it stopped in dropped, and followed by `api::chat::refusal_notice` (B3, SME-122). A reply with nothing but empty or blank text is never saved, and `turn::history_for_request` leaves out any such assistant message saved before: the API refuses one anywhere but last.

**A reply cut off in the middle of a tool call** (SME-126): every provider stops each content block before the `message_delta` that carries `stop_reason`, so `anthropic::stream` keeps a finished call whose input doesn't parse until the stream ends. When the reply was cut off (`stream::is_cut_off`) and its last block is a tool call, that call is dropped, whether or not its input parsed, and `StreamedTurn::cut_off_call` names it, when its name is a plain tool name (the notice is a user-role message the model reads; anything else, or none, is dropped unnamed); the notice says which call it was. It's never run or saved. A cut-off reply's whole calls before it are saved but not run (its `stop_reason` isn't `tool_use`), each with a saved error result (`turn::CUT_OFF_TOOL_CALL`). A reply that was nothing but the cut call saves no assistant message, only the notice. Any other unparseable input (a reply that wasn't cut off, or a call that isn't the last block) still fails the turn.

**A tool call written as text** (SME-286): a provider that doesn't parse a call the model writes (an unknown tool's name, a looser parser than llama.cpp's) returns it as ordinary text. After each reply the turn loop (`turn::reply::after_reply`) looks for call markup in the reply's text blocks, never its thinking, with `text_calls::find`: fenced and indented code, inline code (within a paragraph, so a span can cross a line break; a whole tag call at a line's start, closed by its end tag on a later line, ends the paragraph, so a stray backtick in the prose before it can't hide it) and blockquotes are masked first; reasoning leaked into the text (a `<think>` at a line's start, up to the `</think>` that closes it; one elsewhere, or never closed, is text) is masked too; special-token markers count anywhere else, each only with its form's structure after it (`[TOOL_CALLS]NAME[ARGS]`, `<|tool_call_begin|>…<|tool_call_argument_begin|>`, `<｜tool▁call▁begin｜>NAME<｜tool▁sep｜>`, `<|tool_call>call:NAME{`, `<|tool_call_start|>[NAME(`, `to=functions.NAME<|…`), so prose naming a marker isn't a call; tag markers (`<tool_call>` with `<function=NAME>`, a JSON `"name"` or GLM's bare name; `<function=NAME>` with its arguments; `<function_calls>`/`<invoke name="NAME">`, each with an optional namespace such as `minimax:` or `｜DSML｜`) count only at a line's start. A reply with markup and no real call gets a correction (`text_calls::text_call_notice`, a user-role message like the cut-off notice) naming each call, whether it's offered, and up to five offered tools with similar names (`text_calls::similar`: the named MCP server's tools first, then by shared words weighted by how rare each is among the offered tools, verbs such as add/create/save counted as one; and, for a slip, SME-307: a word of the call with no exact match that is near one of the candidate's, verbs never, counted at half the lighter word's weight, where near is one edit for words of 5 to 7 characters and a third of the characters from 8 up, as `todosote` is from `todowrite`; and a name equal to the candidate's once `_` and `-` are dropped, as `todo_write` is), and the model is called again. After 3 corrections in a row (a real call resets the count) the 4th ends the turn with an error naming the tool; its correction is still saved. A reply that makes a real call and writes one as text too gets the correction after the real calls' results; markup naming a call the reply also made isn't corrected (a provider that left a parsed call's markup in the text: it ran), nor is markup with no name to read (Mistral's JSON list); the match is by name only, so a second, unmade call to a tool the reply also called gets no correction either. A reply with no text and no call — thinking only, empty, or text that is only leaked `<think>` reasoning, since `has_text` decides with the text masked by the shared `text_calls::mask_think` rule and the reply itself saves verbatim (an unclosed `<think>`, and any text after `</think>`, is text; SME-290) — gets a nudge (`text_calls::EMPTY_REPLY_NOTICE`) and the model is called again; another such reply before a real call ends the turn. A nudge, like a real call, resets the correction count. A `stop_reason` of `tool_use` with no call in the reply, when the reply has text, is the reply's end, not an empty set of results. The transcript shows both notices as notice lines (`system_notice`). Not detected: a bare JSON call with no wrapper, Functionary v3.2's `>>>NAME`, and any other form.

**A real call by a name that isn't offered** (SME-307) is corrected the same way, in its tool result: `turn::reply::run_tools` keeps the error the call's run gave (`unknown tool: …`) and adds the sentence the text-call correction uses (`text_calls::unoffered_call_result`): that it isn't an offered tool, and up to five similar offered tools or that none is. The call and its result stay in the history, and no notice line is added. A reply whose every call names an unoffered tool counts toward the same bound as one that writes a call as text, so a model alternating the two still reaches it: the 4th correction of either kind in a row ends the turn (`text_calls::unoffered_bound_error`, naming the reply's first call). Unlike the text-call bound's saved correction, here the 4th reply's calls have run and their results, with the suggestions, are saved before the turn ends, so the next turn reads them. Only an error saying the name is no tool's (`unknown tool: …`, `unknown MCP server: …`, `text_calls::is_unknown_name_error`), or a failed call to a tool an offered MCP server doesn't offer (the crossed prefix, `add_issue_comment` on Linear's server: it reaches the server, since an unlisted tool is callable, SME-237; `text_calls::is_unoffered_tool_of_an_offered_server`), gets the sentence and counts: an offered tool that ran and failed in its own words isn't a slip. Any reply with a call that isn't such a failure resets the count. A slip of a word counts word by word (`todosote` for `todowrite`, `isue` for `issue`), never by a shared verb, so `delete_document` isn't taken for `delete_attachment`. On a Claude Code provider the slip arrives the other way round (SME-447): the CLI refuses a call of a name it was never given locally, and smelt reads that echoed `tool_result` for one of the reply's calls — by the name, never the wording, against the tools this run handed the CLI — as the same slip, not a divergence: the turn carries on, it counts toward this bound, and what smelt saves as the call's result is its own correction, never the CLI's words. A CLI-answered call of a name the CLI *was* given is still the divergence that fails the turn — and so is any echo smelt can't check, a run whose `init` listed no tool names being uncheckable (the arm keeping review 1's divergence test green).
- **No model yet** (a new conversation, one from before providers, or one whose provider was deleted): it takes the default (`db::adopt_default_model`, one statement, which leaves a model the picker set meanwhile alone), keeps it, and publishes `ModelChanged`.
- **No default either:** the turn fails with `providers::NO_MODEL_CONFIGURED`. The chat page shows that with a link to `/providers` rather than as an error, for a `TurnError` and a `NotificationDeliveryFailed` alike; the picker disables Send until there's a model.
- **After a switch** of backend, thinking blocks written before it aren't replayed: their signatures were issued for the other one. The switch is recorded when a turn *starts* on a backend (provider, base URL, model) other than the last turn's (`db::record_turn_model`, under the turn lock), not when the model is picked, since a turn still running keeps writing for the old one; editing a provider's base URL counts too. `history_for_request` drops thinking up to `conversations.model_changed_at_message_id`; a message that was only thinking keeps its reasoning as plain text (the API rejects empty content). A conversation from before providers counts as switched on its first turn, since it may have run on another backend. Nothing stored changes.
- **The context indicator and detail view** show the conversation's model's window, or the default's, without saving anything (`providers::conversation_context_window`).

If a request fails with Ollama's specific "error parsing tool call" 500 (its Anthropic-compat shim, at least for `gpt-oss` models, doesn't always turn a model's raw output into valid tool-call JSON — either because thinking's reasoning landed in the same text as the call, or the model just wrote invalid JSON on its own), `run_turn_bounded` retries: first with `thinking` dropped, then up to `TOOL_CALL_PARSE_RETRIES` further plain regenerations, since a local model's next sampling pass often doesn't repeat the same malformed output. Gives up and surfaces the error once that's exhausted. Any other failure propagates from `run_turn_bounded` immediately. See `is_ollama_thinking_tool_call_corruption`.

One layer down, `anthropic::stream::stream_anthropic_message_metered` retries a briefly unavailable provider on its own: an HTTP 429, 503 or 529 gets two more attempts, 1s and then 3s later (`RETRY_DELAYS`), before anything has streamed. Any other status fails at once. A failure reads `model provider error {status}: {message}`, where the message is the provider's own (`provider_error_message` unwraps the JSON error body, including a nested one), not the raw response.

`run_turn_body` refuses a conversation that doesn't exist with a plain `conversation not found` (checked right after taking the conversation's lock), rather than letting the insert fail on a foreign key; `start_turn` makes the same check before spawning, so `send_message` returns the error itself. `get_messages` returns the same error, which the chat page uses to show "This conversation doesn't exist" instead of a message box. The new message is saved *before* the credential check, so with no credentials configured the message is kept and only the reply fails.

**Some rows are not persisted by the request that "sent" them.** A finished terminal command (`Notice::Wake`) and a notice from outside a turn (`Notice::Deliver`, e.g. Docker restarting or a trust decision), both sent through `turn::notify`, reach the conversation with no `send_message` call in flight at all. A wake pushes its own turn via `run_turn_bounded`, admitted first by `TurnInFlight::start_unless_paused` (counted as in flight, or refused while a Stop's pause holds; SME-217). A `Deliver` is queued as an `agent_messages` row of kind `server_notice` (its marked text, verbatim; SME-450) and the conversation woken: a wake drains it into a turn, or a running turn takes it at its next iteration (D5), and a restart before then keeps it. A paused conversation, or a child that takes no notices, has it saved at once instead (D4); so does one whose wake a Stop ended or a waiting question stopped (D9, D10), with no turn run and no answer owed; if the row can't be queued it's delivered as before, as its own turn's message. Those rows are stored exactly like any other turn (see [models.md](../models.md)), just written outside the request/response cycle a naive reading of this API might assume. See "Live conversation events" below for how a browser tab learns about them.
